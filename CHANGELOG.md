# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Minecraft-style water rendering: water is now a `BlockProperties::translucent` block, drawn in its own alpha-blended pass (`Chunk::draw_water`, `World::draw`) after every chunk's opaque geometry, with depth *tested* but not *written* so solid terrain in front still correctly hides it. `Chunk::build_mesh` builds a separate mesh for translucent faces, and no longer draws the boundary face between two adjacent blocks of the *same* translucent type (e.g. two water blocks) — Minecraft doesn't render the water-water (or glass-glass) interior boundary either. Face tint (`BlockProperties::texture_tints`, `blocks.json`'s `"color"`) now carries a 4th, optional alpha component for exactly this.
- Water's top face (and the top edge of its 4 side faces) now sits 2/16 of a block below a full block's top, same as real Minecraft's still water — `Chunk::append_face` gained a `top_drop` parameter that lowers only a face's own upward-facing corners, applied whenever a Water block has something other than more Water directly above it (an interior, fully-submerged water block's top face is never actually meshed at all, so it doesn't need this). The block grid itself (collision/opacity/light) still treats a water cell as a full block — only the rendered mesh is shorter.
- Water now flows the way real Minecraft's does, with actual source blocks and flowing water rather than an undifferentiated "wet/dry" queue: each Water cell now carries a `Chunk::get_fluid_level`/`set_fluid_level` state (`FLUID_LEVEL_SOURCE`, 1-7 `FLUID_LEVEL_MAX_FLOW` flowing levels counting horizontal distance from the nearest source, `FLUID_LEVEL_FALLING`). A block change (`break_block`/`place_block`) schedules its neighbors for re-evaluation a few ticks later (`World::schedule_fluid_neighbors`/`FLUID_TICK_DELAY`, matching Java Edition's own liquid tick rate) instead of resolving instantly; `World::update_fluids()` — called every tick alongside `update_chunk_states()` — drains whatever's due, recomputing each cell's level purely from its current neighbors (`compute_fluid_level`): water above always wins (falling, no spread-distance limit), otherwise one more than the lowest neighboring level (a source or falling neighbor counting as 0), capped at 7. This is symmetric by construction: an Air cell next to water fills in, and — the real difference from the old system — a FLOWING (never a SOURCE) cell whose feed disappears (its source gets built over, say) dries back up to Air once the resulting re-derivations ripple out and settle, the same self-healing behavior real Minecraft water has. Resolving is capped at 64 due cells per tick (`MAX_FLUID_UPDATES_PER_TICK`) so a large flood or a large drain spreads visibly over time instead of hitching one frame; relighting/remeshing stay batched per affected chunk. Generated terrain water is left alone as all-source (never scheduled) since it's already a settled body of water — only player edits set anything moving.
- Water is animated two ways: `assets/shaders/chunk.fs` scrolls the sampled texel within its own atlas tile (wrapped so it loops seamlessly) whenever `isWaterPass` is set, giving the single static water tile a flowing look without needing extra animation frames; `assets/shaders/chunk.vs` additionally ripples a small two-wave sine displacement into a water surface's own top corners (identified by their fractional local Y, left at .875 by the top_drop above — a fully-submerged water block's top face is never actually meshed, so every vertex reaching this shader during the water pass is unambiguously one of these or a full-height bottom corner), using each vertex's own world position so the ripple lines up seamlessly across chunk borders. `Chunk.hpp`'s new `set_chunk_water_time()`/`set_chunk_water_pass()` feed both shader stages a shared clock and toggle the effects on only around `Chunk::draw_water()`'s own draw calls, called each frame from `World::draw()`.
- Underwater fog, the same way real Minecraft does it: water's own surface color stays blocks.json's one plain tint everywhere, regardless of depth — what actually gets worse with depth is *visibility*. `World::draw` now checks `water_depth_at(camera.position)`, and whenever the camera's eye is inside a Water block, swaps the normal render-distance fog for a much shorter, fluid-colored one (`UNDERWATER_FOG_*`) in its place, shortening and darkening further the deeper the camera is (toward `UNDERWATER_FOG_MAX_DEPTH`) instead of one fixed fog regardless of depth. Also disables backface culling for `Chunk::draw_water()`'s own pass — without it, a water surface's top face (wound to face up) would vanish when looked at from *underneath*, letting the raw, unfogged sky show through a "hole" in what should be an enclosed, foggy underwater view. Ocean's own floor (`biome_terrain`) is now noticeably deeper (world Y ~10-30, was ~30-46) so there's real deep water for the fog to reach its darkest/shortest end on.
- Water now slows movement through it: `GameObject::update`/`Entity::update` gained a `const World*` parameter (threaded through from `GameEngine`'s own object list, and now `Player::update` too) so `Entity::update` can halve applied movement (`WATER_DRAG`) whenever `World::water_depth_at` says the entity's position is submerged — deliberately not applied to `GameEngine`'s own free-look camera, which is a spectator-style camera with no collision of its own, not an `Entity`.
- The player now spawns properly instead of at a fixed height-agnostic point: `World::find_spawn_position` searches outward from the origin in expanding rings for a column that's dry land (never Sea/Ocean) with clear air above the ground to actually appear in, generating whatever chunk a promising candidate needs to confirm it before accepting it — in practice this resolves on the first ring or two, since land covers roughly half the world. `GameEngine::set_world` places the camera there (plus `CAMERA_EYE_HEIGHT` above it, Minecraft's own player eye height) facing exactly north (-Z in this engine's convention) at a level pitch, rather than the old bird's-eye starting view. Getting the facing exactly right needed one more fix: `DisableCursor()` capturing the mouse registers as a couple of frames of spurious `GetMouseDelta()` jump (measured: a jump from wherever the OS cursor physically was to the window's center, then a second smaller one) that would otherwise rotate the very first frames away from that orientation — `GameEngine`'s new `spawn_settle_frames` skips applying mouse rotation for the few frames right after spawning to absorb it.
- `Clay` block (`BlockType::Clay`, `core/Block.hpp`/`.cpp`): small patches of it now generate within underwater Sand (a Desert dipping below sea level, or a calm Sea beach) wherever a new small-scale `TerrainNoise::clay` noise field crosses above `CLAY_THRESHOLD` (`Chunk::generate_terrain`) — the same idea as real Minecraft's shallow-water clay deposits, high frequency keeping each patch a handful of blocks rather than covering the whole beach. Only ever replaces the surface block itself, not what's under it, so a patch reads as a thin deposit sitting in the sand.
- Forest's own terrain (`biome_terrain`) is noticeably smoother now (`height_variation` 10, was 18) — a gentler roll, closer to Plains, with fewer/smaller hills than before.
- Rivers now tunnel underground instead of always carving an open valley: `Chunk::generate_terrain` fades the surface-carving strength itself from full (an open valley, banks sloping down to `RIVER_BED`) down to none as the natural (pre-river) terrain rises from `RIVER_BED`'s own level up to `RIVER_TUNNEL_DEPTH` (10) blocks above it, while a flooded channel (`RIVER_TUNNEL_HALF_HEIGHT` blocks tall) is *always* carved through the rock at `RIVER_BED`'s own elevation regardless — it simply has no visible effect wherever the (still at least partly lowered) surface already reaches that far down. Fading the two continuously like this, rather than switching all-or-nothing at one fixed depth, matters in practice: real terrain crosses a single fixed depth repeatedly along a winding river, and the first version of this that used a hard cutoff made the river flicker between a visible valley and a fully hidden tunnel every few blocks — reading as scattered points from above rather than one continuous line.
- Underground caves, Minecraft Beta 1.7.3-style: `Chunk::carve_caves` (called from `World::generate_chunk`, right after `generate_terrain`) carves winding tunnels using the "Perlin worms" random-walk approach that version actually used (superseded in modern Minecraft by 3D noise-density caves) — a tunnel's yaw/pitch drift step to step from an accumulated "velocity" nudged randomly each step, rather than being repicked fresh, with pitch decaying back toward level over time; each step carves a tapered ellipsoid (radius easing in/out via a sine curve along the tunnel's length) into whatever it passes through, occasionally widening into a single larger cavern instead of a normal tunnel. Never carves through Air, Water, or Bedrock. Each origin chunk's tunnels are seeded purely from `(world_seed, chunk_x, chunk_z)` (`cave_chunk_seed`, `Chunk.cpp`), so a tunnel that crosses a chunk border still carves identically regardless of which chunk actually generates first. `CAVE_CHUNK_RADIUS`/`CAVE_CHUNK_RARITY` were tuned empirically after an initial attempt at Beta's actual per-chunk tunnel-count formula turned out to average far more tunnels than intended (~9x slower initial world load, ~80% of underground columns hitting open air) — the tuned constants land closer to ~12% underground-column coverage with a much smaller load-time cost.
- Ravines: alongside normal cave tunnels, `Chunk::carve_caves` now also occasionally carves a ravine — the same "Perlin worm" walk, but narrow side-to-side and stretched much taller (a canyon-like gash rather than a round tunnel), wandering far less than a cave tunnel and biased toward starting closer to the surface. Rolled on its own separate rarity (`RAVINE_CHUNK_RARITY`, salted independently from the cave roll so the two aren't the same coin flip) so it stays a rare, striking find rather than a common feature. Verified by scanning generated terrain for tall, narrow vertical air shafts (height >= 8 blocks, width <= 6) distinct from any round cave chamber — found one 32 blocks tall and only 5 wide.
- Sand and Gravel now fall the way real Minecraft's do: `World::update_falling_blocks()` — called every tick alongside `update_chunk_states()`/`update_fluids()` — moves an unsupported Sand or Gravel block down exactly one cell (falling through water, not stopped by it, the same as vanilla), then reschedules that new position for the *next* tick if it's still unsupported, so a drop reads as a steady one-block-per-tick fall rather than an instant teleport to wherever it lands. `break_block()` schedules the cell directly above whatever was just removed (in case it was resting on that block); `place_block()` schedules a placed Sand/Gravel block's own cell (in case it was placed over open space). A falling block also notifies nearby water the same way `break_block`/`place_block` do, and notifies the cell above its own old position in turn, so a whole stack losing its support falls one level at a time rather than only the bottom block reacting.
- Debug (F4): `World::draw_chunk_borders`, a magenta wireframe box around every loaded chunk's full column — makes the chunk grid itself visible regardless of terrain.
- Debug (F5): wireframe ("skeleton") view — `GameEngine::draw` wraps `World::draw` in `rlEnableWireMode()`/`rlDisableWireMode()` instead of adding a separate rendering path, so it's always exactly whatever mesh `Chunk::build_mesh` actually produced, just as GL_LINE edges instead of filled/textured triangles.
- `assets/` folder (`sprites`, `sounds`, `fonts`) for game resources, and `CREDITS.md` with attribution for third-party art assets.
- `GameObject` base class with `WorldObject` (walls, floor, doors, windows) and `Entity` (players, mobs, items) subclasses.
- `core/` folders (`src/core`, `include/core`) for engine-level system code; `GameEngine` (`core/`) owns the window, the main loop, and the `GameObject` list, so `main.cpp` just creates and runs it.
- Voxel engine core: `Block`/`Chunk` data model with a JSON-driven block registry (`assets/blocks.json`), per-face chunk rendering with Minecraft-style vertex AO, two-channel (sky + block) BFS light propagation, a free-look debug camera, and a gradient skybox.
- `TextureManager`, a shared texture cache used by any system that loads textures (replaces the block-only `BlockTextures`).
- Perlin-noise terrain generation (`core/PerlinNoise`) covering a 512x512-block world (32x32 chunks).
- `FontManager`, a shared font cache (mirrors `TextureManager`) — every in-game text now draws with `assets/fonts/Minecraft Rus/minecraft.ttf` (loaded with Cyrillic glyphs, for Russian text) instead of raylib's built-in default font.
- Per-face tint color in `blocks.json` (`BlockProperties::texture_tints`): a face can multiply a color into its tile, for art that's deliberately colorless and meant to be recolored in code (e.g. grass top).
- Each chunk now builds into a single cached GPU mesh (`Chunk::build_mesh`) instead of per-face immediate-mode drawing, with faces hidden by an opaque neighbor culled out — including across chunk borders.
- `World` class (`World.hpp`/`.cpp`): owns the chunk grid and everything that needs to reach across chunk borders — generation order, camera raycasting, and block edits.
- Block breaking: left-click removes the block the camera is aimed at, up to 10 blocks away (voxel DDA raycast in `World::raycast`), relighting and remeshing the edited chunk and its neighbors.
- Minecraft-style crosshair: a "+" at screen center, rendered with an inverted-color blend so it stays visible over any background.
- Debug overlay (F3, Minecraft-style): FPS, position, block/chunk coordinates, facing, light level, fly speed, and the block currently targeted.
- `get_block_name()` (`core/Block`): a block's `blocks.json` name, for the debug overlay's "Looking at" line.
- Mouse wheel adjusts the free-look camera's fly speed (2-100 blocks/second), Minecraft creative/spectator-style.
- Block placing: right-click places a block against the targeted face, up to 15 blocks away (`World::place_block`); no inventory yet, so it always places Cobblestone.
- Block-selection outline: a black wireframe box around whatever block the crosshair is aimed at, out to the block-placing distance (15 blocks).
- Biomes (`core/Biome`): Plains/Forest/Desert/Hills, in the spirit of Minecraft Beta 1.7.3's original biome set trimmed to what this project has matching blocks for. `TerrainNoise` (`core/TerrainNoise`, replacing the bare `PerlinNoise` `World` used to hold directly) bundles the height noise with a temperature and a humidity noise map (both very low frequency, so biomes span whole regions) — `classify_biome` picks a biome from temperature/humidity alone, a Whittaker-diagram-style lookup independent of the terrain height about to be generated, same as Beta's own approach. Each biome then gets its own height range and surface/subsurface blocks (`BiomeTerrain` in `Chunk.cpp`) — Desert is flat and sandy, Hills is the roughest terrain generated (deliberately still gentler than modern Minecraft's Extreme Hills, since Beta 1.7.3 predates that terrain rework), Plains and Forest sit in between. `World::get_biome` exposes the same classification for the debug overlay, which now shows a "Biome:" line.
- Fixed a real bug found while adding biomes: FastNoise2's `Perlin` node silently divides every input coordinate by its own default "feature scale" (100, i.e. a hidden x0.01 on frequency) before sampling, on top of whatever frequency `PerlinNoise::noise()`/`fractal()`'s own caller already multiplies in. This made the biome temperature/humidity noise (sampled with `noise()`) come back nearly constant — caught immediately since it broke biome variety outright — but terrain height (sampled with `fractal()`, whose source is the same kind of node) was silently affected the exact same way the whole time: what looked like plausible rolling hills was actually a ~100x-too-large wavelength, i.e. a tiny slice of one giant, nearly-flat slope, not distinct ~96-block hills. `PerlinNoise`'s constructor now calls `SetScale(1.0f)` so its node does no scaling of its own — terrain now shows real, repeating rolling hills at the intended wavelength instead.
- Fixed-rate game tick (`GameEngine::tick`, 20/second, Minecraft's own tick rate) via an accumulator in `GameEngine::run`, decoupled from the render frame rate and capped against a lag-spike "spiral of death"; the running tick count shows on the debug overlay. Nothing simulates on it yet — it's the clock future world systems (day/night, scheduled block updates, random ticks) will run on.
- Dynamic chunk loading: `ChunkState` (`Unloaded`/`Loaded`/`Active`, `Chunk.hpp`) plus `World::update_chunk_states`, called every tick with the camera's position — chunks within a configurable `LOADED_RADIUS` are generated/meshed on demand and drawn, chunks within the smaller `ACTIVE_RADIUS` inside that are additionally flagged as ticking (no simulation reads the flag yet — it's the extension point for future per-chunk gameplay), and anything that falls out of `LOADED_RADIUS` is unloaded (mesh and block data freed; a modified chunk's edits aren't persisted yet — `World::unload_chunk` has a TODO). `World` no longer generates its whole grid up front, so a chunk's state now genuinely starts at `Unloaded`. Transition logic (`desired_state_for`, `load_chunk`, `unload_chunk`) is kept separate from the pure world/chunk data it acts on, as a seam for a future client/server split.
- `Water` block (`assets/blocks.json`, non-solid, transparent), filling every column from its terrain height up to `WATER_LEVEL` (block 64, `Chunk.cpp`) — a sea level, same as Minecraft's own.
- Debug overlay's "Chunk:" line now also shows position local to the chunk (not just which chunk), matching Minecraft's own F3 "local x/y/z in chunk x/z" format.

### Changed
- Renamed class methods project-wide to snake_case; moved `Json`, `GameObject`, `IsoCamera`, and `Block` into `core/`.
- Block textures now index a fixed 16x16 grid of tiles in `assets/sprites/terrain.png` by `{x, y}` coordinates per face (`blocks.json`'s `top`/`bottom`/`side` are now `{x, y}` objects, with an optional `color` tint, instead of sprite filenames), rather than dynamically packing individual sprite files into an atlas at startup; removed the now-unused `core/TextureAtlas`.
- `core/PerlinNoise` is now backed by FastNoise2 (`external/FastNoise2`, a new git submodule) instead of a hand-rolled implementation — same public API (`PerlinNoise(seed)`, `.noise()`/`.fractal()`), SIMD-accelerated noise underneath, with native Apple Silicon (NEON) support.
- Chunks are now `CHUNK_SIZE` (16) wide/deep but `CHUNK_HEIGHT` (256) tall — a single chunk per (x, z) column spans the whole world height (still no vertical chunk *stacking* — that's a different thing) instead of being a 16x16x16 cube. Every bound check and loop that used to reuse `CHUNK_SIZE` for the Y axis now uses `CHUNK_HEIGHT`. Terrain generation's `BASE_HEIGHT`/`HEIGHT_VARIATION` (`Chunk.cpp`) were raised to fit this taller world and sit mostly above the new water level, and the starting camera position/angle (`GameEngine.cpp`) were retuned to clear the now much taller (and steeper) hills.
- Performance: `Chunk::build_mesh`/`compute_lighting` now stop at each chunk's actual highest block (`highest_block_y`, tracked by `set_block`) instead of always walking the full `CHUNK_HEIGHT` (256) — most of that is guaranteed air above real terrain. `World::update_chunk_states` now generates/unloads every chunk a call needs first and meshes whatever that actually touched exactly once at the end, instead of remeshing each chunk's 3x3 neighborhood immediately per chunk — the initial world population was doing 1345 mesh rebuilds for 289 chunks (~4.6x redundant); batching cut the same initial load from 1.17s to 0.25s. Removed the now-redundant const/non-const duplicate of `World::chunk_at`'s body.
- World height is now `CHUNK_HEIGHT` (384) blocks, world Y -64..319, matching Minecraft 1.18+ (was 0..255) — `MIN_WORLD_Y` (`Chunk.hpp`) is the new world-space Y of a chunk's local index 0; `Chunk` itself stays plainly 0-based internally, same as X/Z, with `World` doing the +/-64 translation at its public API boundary (`get_block`/`get_light`/`set_block_and_rebuild`/`generate_chunk`). Terrain generation's local constants were recalibrated so the actual world-space terrain/water height didn't change.
- `World` no longer takes a fixed `size_in_blocks` (removed from its constructor and `main.cpp`) — it has no size at all now, just a very large `WORLD_BORDER_BLOCKS` (8,000,000, `World.cpp`) chunks are allowed to generate out to, technically bounded but practically infinite, the same idea as Minecraft's own world border. Deliberately not Minecraft's actual 29,999,984: this project stores positions in 32-bit floats (raylib's `Vector3`), which can only represent every integer exactly up to 2^24 (~16.7M) — going further would mean visible block jitter long before the border, without also switching to double-precision or camera-relative positions (a separate, bigger change). The starting camera now spawns near world origin (0, 0), Minecraft's own convention, instead of the center of the old fixed 512x512 area.
- Performance: `World::draw` now skips chunks the camera clearly isn't looking toward (`chunk_in_view`, an approximate view-cone test — not exact frustum-plane culling, to avoid the risk of getting raylib's matrix convention wrong and silently hiding things that should be visible) — roughly halves how many chunks issue a draw call on a given frame (136 of 289 in one measurement) without changing which chunks are loaded/simulated.
- Distance fog: `Chunk`'s shared mesh material now uses a custom shader (`assets/shaders/chunk.vs`/`.fs` — raylib's own default mesh shader plus a linear fog blend; loaded once by `load_chunk_shader()` in `GameEngine`'s constructor) instead of raylib's plain-texture default. `World::draw` sets `fogColor`/`fogStart`/`fogEnd`/`cameraPosition` each frame (`set_chunk_fog`, `Chunk.hpp`) — `fogColor` matches the skybox's own horizon color (`skybox_horizon_color()`, `Skybox.hpp`) so the render-distance edge reads as fading into the sky; `fogEnd` is 80% of `LOADED_RADIUS`'s own distance and `fogStart` 50% of that, so a chunk unloading at the actual edge does so already fully inside the fog.
- Biome transitions are now smoothly blended instead of a hard cutoff: `classify_biome` (temperature/humidity -> one biome) is replaced by `compute_biome_weights` (`core/Biome`), returning a `BiomeWeights` (one float per biome, summing to 1) built from smoothstep ramps instead of single-point thresholds, so crossing a boundary changes gradually over the ramp's width instead of at a seam. `Chunk::generate_terrain` now blends every biome's own height range/amplitude by these weights instead of picking just one biome's numbers outright, so terrain height itself changes gradually across a border too, not only which surface block shows; grass tint blends the same way (see below). `dominant_biome` (the highest-weight biome) is still what the debug overlay's "Biome:" line and per-column surface/subsurface block choice use. Biomes are also noticeably bigger now: `BIOME_FREQUENCY` (900, was 600) and `CONTINENT_FREQUENCY` (1800, was 1200, `TerrainNoise.hpp`) both dropped, so each region spans more world before the next one takes over.
- Sea and Ocean are now two separate biomes sharing one land-vs-water axis (`continentalness`, via `continent_noise`) instead of one: Sea is the shallow, calm band close to shore (world Y ~56-64), Ocean the deep, "wild" band further out (world Y ~30-46) — both floored in gravel. Which band a given stretch of coastline falls into isn't continentalness alone, though: a new, smaller-scale `coast_noise` layer (`TerrainNoise::COAST_FREQUENCY`) independently decides whether *this* stretch of coast is calm (buffered by Sea before the water gets deep) or wild (drops straight to Ocean at the shore), so both kinds of coastline occur side by side rather than the whole map picking one.
- Beach: land within a few blocks of sea level, close enough to Sea or Ocean to matter, now gets a shoreline material instead of its own biome's usual surface block (`Chunk::generate_terrain`) — sand next to a calm Sea coastline, gravel next to a "wild" Ocean one, matching each water biome's own character. Combined with the biome blending above, this reads as a gentle, plains-like slope down into a Sea, or a rockier drop next to Ocean.
- Rivers: a new `river_noise` layer (`TerrainNoise::river`) carves a winding channel wherever its value crosses near zero, the same zero-contour idea the coastline itself uses — near Desert's own biome-weight transition zone (`Chunk::generate_terrain`), so a river specifically separates Desert from its neighboring biome rather than cutting through every biome pair, and also near any Sea/Ocean coastline, so a river's course keeps carving as it reaches the coast and visibly empties into the sea instead of stopping dead at the biome border.
- Per-biome grass tint now blends continuously instead of switching at whichever point `dominant_biome()` flips: `grass_tint_for_weights` (`Chunk.cpp`) averages Plains' lighter green against Forest/Hills' existing tint by how much each actually influences a column's `BiomeWeights`, cached per-column in `Chunk::column_grass_tint` (replacing the old per-column `Biome` cache) and used in place of `blocks.json`'s one fixed tint for every Grass block. Hills' tallest peaks (above `HILLS_STONE_LINE`, world Y 95) still surface as bare stone instead of grass, a simple tree-line effect; Desert's surface is sand all the way through its (deeper than other biomes') subsurface layer instead of only the very top block.

### Fixed
- Grass generating underwater: a land column's own blended terrain height (`Chunk::generate_terrain`) can still dip below `WATER_LEVEL` from height noise alone, not just near an actual coastline (where the existing beach override already replaces it with sand/gravel) — that surface now becomes Dirt instead of Grass, same as real Minecraft, where a grass block needs open air/sunlight above it and reverts to dirt without that.
- Startup crash on block names in `blocks.json` with no matching `BlockType` entry yet (new ore/wood types added without updating the enum).
- AO and smooth lighting at chunk borders: `Chunk::build_mesh` now samples real neighbor-chunk block/light data (including diagonal neighbors, for corner vertices) instead of chunk-edge defaults, removing the seam that was visible along chunk boundaries.
- Skybox not rendering: flipping depth-test/backface-culling state right after drawing it didn't flush the pending render batch first, so its quads were rasterized later with the wrong GL state and culled away.
